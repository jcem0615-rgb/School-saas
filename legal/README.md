# Legal templates

Three documents a school will ask for before it signs, drafted from what
this system actually does rather than from a generic template.

| File | Between | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `data-processing-agreement.md` | LogicGrid and the school | What the operator may do with the school's data, and what it must do about a breach |
| `privacy-notice-template.md` | the school and its families | The school's own notice, which its families read |
| `retention-schedule.md` | internal | How long each kind of record is kept, and why |

## Read this first

**These are drafts to take to a lawyer, not legal advice, and nobody
here is one.** They are written to be a useful starting point — the
facts in them about what the software collects, who can read it and how
it is protected are accurate, and those facts are the part a lawyer
cannot supply. The clauses around them are ordinary and unremarkable and
should be reviewed by counsel before anybody signs.

Every place a decision is yours is marked `[LIKE THIS]`. Do not send one
of these to a school with the brackets still in it.

## Who is who

Under Philippine data protection law the roles matter and they are not
interchangeable:

- **The school is the Personal Information Controller.** It decides what
  it collects about its students and why. It owns the relationship with
  the family, it answers to the family, and its name is on the privacy
  notice.
- **LogicGrid is the Personal Information Processor.** It holds and
  processes that information on the school's instructions. It does not
  decide what is collected, does not use it for anything of its own, and
  does not share it.

This is why there are two documents rather than one. The agreement binds
the operator to the school; the notice is what the school says to its
families. A processor that writes the school's privacy notice for it has
misunderstood which of them is answerable.
